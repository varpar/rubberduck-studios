# Case-study questions

These questions are designed to turn the existing portfolio pages into credible, detailed case studies without inventing outcomes. Short bullet answers, voice notes, links, and rough screenshots are all useful. If something is confidential, say so—we can describe the decision without exposing the data.

## Shared project context

1. What was the original brief, and what made the project worth doing at that moment?
2. Who commissioned it, who was on the delivery team, and what was Rubberduck responsible for?
3. What were the biggest constraints: timeline, budget, technical platform, brand rules, approvals, or existing content?
4. What did success look like before work began? What had to feel different for a customer, visitor, or internal team?
5. Which decisions are you most proud of, and why were they hard to make?
6. What shipped, what remains planned, and when did the work launch?

## NutriPanda — commerce system

1. Who is the buyer, what are they trying to solve, and what was confusing or difficult about the previous purchase journey?
2. What made the product range distinct, and what did a visitor need to understand before buying?
3. Which parts did Rubberduck design and build: brand direction, storefront, product content, checkout, delivery logic, dashboard, integrations, or ongoing operations?
4. What did research, customer conversations, founder input, or competitor review reveal about the buying decision?
5. Can you describe one important product-page decision—for example ingredients, claims, trust signals, subscriptions, bundles, dosage, or comparison—and the reasoning behind it?
6. What are the real checkout rules: payment methods, serviceable areas, shipping thresholds, COD/prepaid choices, coupons, taxes, order confirmation, and support hand-off?
7. Which day-to-day jobs can the operating layer now handle, and what did that workflow look like before it existed?
8. What is the technical stack and which integrations are safe to name publicly?
9. Are there any before/after numbers, even directional ones: conversion, checkout completion, support requests, time to update stock or prices, order-processing time, revenue, repeat purchase, or product launch speed?
10. May we use real product, checkout, and dashboard screens? Please flag any customer, order, address, or internal data that must be removed.

## Mi Amor Tales — editorial wedding experience

1. Who is Mi Amor Tales trying to attract, and what should a prospective couple feel in the first thirty seconds on the site?
2. What was not working about the former site or the way the studio presented its work?
3. What was the creative brief for the visual direction: references, words to embody, and things to avoid?
4. Which parts did Rubberduck own: strategy, art direction, copy, UX, design, development, animation, performance, content system, or launch support?
5. How were individual stories structured? What is intentionally consistent across a wedding page, and what can change to suit a couple or location?
6. What role should stills, films, rituals, captions, music, and enquiry play in the story flow?
7. What did the mobile experience need to protect or simplify compared with desktop?
8. What technical or content challenges were solved: image delivery, video hosting, page speed, accessibility, content updates, direct links, or SEO?
9. Do you have client feedback, enquiry-quality changes, time-on-site, portfolio views, bookings, or other results we can quote with context?
10. Do we have permission from the studio and couples to use every image, film poster, name, and testimonial in a public portfolio case study?

## Evidence, assets, and permissions

1. Please share the strongest source materials: original brief, notes, Figma links, early wireframes, before/after screens, launch posts, production screenshots, and client feedback.
2. Which work can be shown publicly, which needs anonymising, and which cannot be shown at all?
3. Who should be credited for photography, video, brand, illustration, development, and any collaborators?
4. Are there quotes we may attribute to a founder, client, or customer? Please include their preferred name/title and explicit approval.
5. Which live links, launch dates, awards, press, or public results may we link to?

## Optional impact data

Numbers are helpful only when they are real and can be contextualised. For each one, share the date range, baseline, source, and whether it is safe to publish.

- Conversion rate, completed orders, average order value, repeat purchase, or checkout drop-off
- Enquiry volume/quality, booked calls, portfolio or film engagement, and organic search growth
- Operational time saved, error reduction, speed of pricing/stock updates, or fewer support tickets
- Performance and accessibility improvements: load time, Core Web Vitals, Lighthouse scores, or device coverage
- Qualitative proof: a client quote, an internal adoption story, or a customer observation

Reply in two sections—**NutriPanda** and **Mi Amor Tales**—with any answers you have, then attach links/screenshots. We can mark unknowns as “not for publication” and shape the finished copy around the strongest verified evidence.
